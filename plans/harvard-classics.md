# The Harvard Classics — Fifteen Minutes a Day

A daily reading plan from *Fifteen Minutes a Day: The Reading Guide* to the Harvard Classics, the 50-volume "five-foot shelf" edited by Charles W. Eliot. Each day pairs a short reading (about fifteen minutes) with an anniversary and a teaser. The guide is in the public domain; the hooks below are paraphrased.

Volume and page numbers refer to the original P. F. Collier volumes. The texts are widely available free online; in another edition, find the passage by its content.

Go at your own pace and track your place by entry, not by calendar date. Starting at January 1 in October is a perfectly good way to do it.

**Contributing:** January is transcribed so far. To add a month, follow the same format, one block per day.

## January

### Jan 1 · Franklin's Advice for the New Year
Franklin, *Autobiography* — Vol. 1, pp. 79–85
Hook: Franklin's virtue of Resolution: resolve to do what you ought, then do what you resolved.

### Jan 2 · School-Day Poems of John Milton
Milton, *Poems* — Vol. 4, pp. 7–18
Hook: verses Milton wrote as a schoolboy, already hinting at what was coming.
Note: first edition of Milton's collected poems published Jan. 2, 1645

### Jan 3 · Cicero on Friendship
Cicero, *On Friendship* — Vol. 9, pp. 16–26
Hook: Rome's great orator ranks friendship as useful as fire and water.
Note: Cicero born Jan. 3, 106 B.C.

### Jan 4 · A Flounder Fish Story
Grimm, *Fairy Tales* — Vol. 17, pp. 83–90
Hook: a fisherman frees a talking flounder, and his wife keeps demanding more miracles.
Note: Jacob Grimm born Jan. 4, 1785

### Jan 5 · The Soaring Eagle and Contented Stork
Mazzini, *Byron and Goethe* — Vol. 32, pp. 377–396
Hook: the exiled Italian patriot compares Byron to a soaring eagle and Goethe to a contented stork.
Note: Byron arrived in Greece to fight for Greek freedom, Jan. 5, 1824

### Jan 6 · Warned by Hector's Ghost
Virgil, *Aeneid* — Vol. 13, pp. 109–127
Hook: Hector's ghost warns Aeneas to flee doomed Troy, carrying his father and leading his son.
Note: H. Schliemann, discoverer of ancient Troy, born Jan. 6, 1822

### Jan 7 · If He Yawned, She Lost Her Head!
*The Thousand and One Nights* — Vol. 16, pp. 5–13
Hook: a sultan executes each new bride at dawn, until Scheherazade starts telling stories.

### Jan 8 · Trying the Patience of Job
*The Book of Job* — Vol. 44, pp. 71–87
Hook: Satan claims Job is pious only because he prospers, and the trial begins.

### Jan 9 · A Treasure Hunt in Nombre de Dios
Nichols, *Sir Francis Drake Revived* — Vol. 33, pp. 135–145
Hook: Drake and fifty-two men go after Spain's treasure at Nombre de Dios.
Note: Drake died at Nombre de Dios, Jan. 9, 1596 (per the guide)

### Jan 10 · Where Love Lies Waiting
Euripides, *The Bacchae* — Vol. 8, pp. 368–372
Hook: King Pentheus of Thebes sets himself against the god Dionysus, who has bewitched the city's women.

### Jan 11 · Hamilton—Father of Wall Street
*The Federalist* — Vol. 43, pp. 199–207
Hook: Hamilton, the Treasury, and the papers that helped bring New York into the Union.
Note: Alexander Hamilton born Jan. 11, 1757

### Jan 12 · What Is Good Taste?
Burke, *On Taste* — Vol. 24, pp. 11–26
Hook: a sultan spots one gruesome flaw in a painting of John the Baptist's severed head. Good judge of art, or not?
Note: Edmund Burke born Jan. 12, 1729

### Jan 13 · Rousseau Seeks Sanctuary in England
Rousseau, *Inquiry on Inequality* — Vol. 34, pp. 215–228
Hook: Rousseau traces human inequality back to our primitive beginnings, ideas that drove him into exile.
Note: Rousseau arrived in England, Jan. 13, 1766

### Jan 14 · The First Step Toward Independence
*The Fundamental Orders of Connecticut* — Vol. 43, pp. 60–65
Hook: billed as the first written constitution that limits government's power.
Note: adopted Jan. 14, 1639

### Jan 15 · "The Moving Finger Writes"
*The Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam* — Vol. 41, pp. 943–953
Hook: a philosophy of enjoying life's good things, the classic book to dip into.
Note: FitzGerald's *Rubaiyat* first published Jan. 15, 1859

### Jan 16 · The Old Woman and the Wine Jar
*Aesop's Fables* — Vol. 17, pp. 43–44; also pp. 31–43
Hook: an old woman sniffs an empty wine jar, and remembers.

### Jan 17 · Franklin's Family Tree
Franklin, *Autobiography* — Vol. 1, pp. 5–15
Hook: the youngest son of the youngest son, for five generations.
Note: Benjamin Franklin born Jan. 17, 1706

### Jan 18 · Origin of Yale "Brekekekex-Ko-ax"
Aristophanes, *The Frogs* — Vol. 8, pp. 439–449
Hook: Dionysus and his slave Xanthias open with a comic double act, and a chorus of frogs gave Yale a cheer.

### Jan 19 · Poe on Poetry
Poe, *The Poetic Principle* — Vol. 28, pp. 371–380
Hook: Poe takes apart what poetry is made of.
Note: Edgar Allan Poe born Jan. 19, 1809

### Jan 20 · "Ah! It Is St. Agnes' Eve—"
Keats, *The Eve of St. Agnes* — Vol. 41, pp. 883–893
Hook: on St. Agnes' Eve, maidens are promised visions of their lovers, and Porphyro makes use of the custom.
Note: St. Agnes' Eve, Jan. 20

### Jan 21 · The Nightingale's Healing Melody
Andersen, *Tales* — Vol. 17, pp. 301–310
Hook: a dying emperor, Death at the bedside, and a bird's song.

### Jan 22 · A King's Pleasure Now Yours
Corneille, *Polyeucte* — Vol. 26, pp. 77–87
Hook: French classical drama, written to please kings.
Note: Corneille elected to the French Academy, Jan. 22, 1647

### Jan 23 · Pascal Knew Men and Triangles
Pascal, *The Art of Persuasion* — Vol. 48, pp. 400–411
Hook: a mathematician measures human conceit as precisely as a triangle.
Note: Pascal published the "Provincial Letters," Jan. 23, 1656

### Jan 24 · Odysseus Silenced the Sirens
Homer, *Odyssey* — Vol. 22, pp. 165–173
Hook: wax in the crew's ears, and Odysseus bound to the mast so he can listen.

### Jan 25 · A Field Mouse Made Famous
Burns, "To a Mouse" and other poems — Vol. 6, pp. 119–120, 388–394
Hook: a ploughman apologizes to the mouse whose nest he turned up.
Note: Robert Burns born Jan. 25, 1759

### Jan 26 · In the Cradle of Civilization
Herodotus, *An Account of Egypt* — Vol. 33, pp. 65–75
Hook: old Egypt as gossip: sacred bulls and cats, court scandal, a daughter entombed in a golden cow.

### Jan 27 · Dante and Beatrice in Paradise
Dante, *Divine Comedy* — Vol. 20, pp. 267–279
Hook: the woman he loved at first sight, and may never have spoken to, meets him in Paradise.
Note: Dante a victim of political persecution in Florence, Jan. 27, 1302

### Jan 28 · Man's Wings
Thomas à Kempis — Vol. 7, pp. 242–249
Hook: simplicity and purity as the wings that lift us above earthly things.

### Jan 29 · Visits the Land of Fire
Darwin, *The Voyage of the Beagle* — Vol. 29, pp. 209–221
Hook: Darwin's study of Tierra del Fuego and its people.
Note: Darwin married Emma Wedgwood, Jan. 29, 1839

### Jan 30 · First Problem Play Popular
Sophocles, *Antigone* — Vol. 8, pp. 255–266
Hook: Antigone defies a king's decree out of duty to her brother. What duty?
Note: Sophocles died at Athens, Jan. 30, 405 B.C.

### Jan 31 · What "Don Quixote" Really Slew
Cervantes, *Don Quixote* — Vol. 14, pp. 60–67
Hook: an amateur knight sets out with windmills, damsels, and a great deal of satire ahead of him.
